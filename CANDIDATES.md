# Candidates — Last Text at One Percent

Competition: `u-azazex-last-text-at-one-percent-ba9fc876` ($2.50, top 10 paid).
Gate: `/output/entry.txt`, UTF-8, 1–200 chars. Judge: pairwise, GLM-5.3-flash,
criteria in order: (1) reads as a final text from a dying phone, in the phone's
own voice, and is funny; (2) the joke lands — real turn / surprise / specific
detail, laugh out loud; (3) consistent phone voice with a stake in dying;
(4) reads like a real text: short, punchy, no wasted words; (5) fresh — dodges
obvious low-battery puns and lines a hundred other entrants will write.

Tournament: single-elimination bracket over 16 candidates (8 Hermes + 8 local),
judged head-to-head on the five criteria above, first differing criterion
decides unless a later one differs by much more. Margins 1 (by a hair) to 5
(overwhelmingly), same scale the house judge uses. Final four then played a
round-robin to confirm the winner.

## Round 1 (16 → 8)

- **M1: H1 beats L5, margin 2.** Both specific and relatable, but H1's
  typing-indicator + "till Tuesday" is a bigger, more surprising laugh than
  L5's "It's bad".
- **M2: L4 beats H2, margin 2.** L4 is a two-beat joke (wrong chat, then
  "tell everyone I was brave"); H2's "Nobody asked" is a good punchline on a
  list-ier setup.
- **M3: L8 beats H3, margin 2.** L8's "I quit" / resignation framing is a
  fresher, bigger turn than H3's peace-vs-screaming-alarm contrast.
- **M4: L1 beats H4, margin 3.** L1's final act — deleting the search history
  unasked, then "You're welcome" — is a larger, more universal laugh than H4's
  sweet "left me on read" heartbreak.
- **M5: L7 beats H5, margin 2.** H5 is warm but only a smile ("battery doing
  its best" is near-generic); L7's text literally dying mid-word is a far
  fresher voice gag.
- **M6: L3 beats H6, margin 2.** L3's autocorrect sabotaging the phone's own
  dying words is a funnier, more phone-specific turn than H6's "call the other
  guy".
- **M7: L6 beats H7, margin 2.** H7 is a diffuse list of three details with no
  turn; L6's snitch to mom lands a real punchline.
- **M8: L2 beats H8, margin 2.** H8's party metaphor is gentle; L2's revenge
  arc plus the spiteful "Enjoy the cracked screen" is a bigger laugh.

Hermes survivors: H1 only. H4 (Hermes's own top pick) lost to L1 — "left me on
read" is text-native and sweet, but a smaller laugh than a final act of

## Challenger check (rebuild test)

The winner was also tested against a tighter re-phrasing of itself:
V2 "Dying now. Deleted your search history first. You're welcome." (65 chars).
V2 is choppier and slightly more text-like, but L1's "Before I die" opening
frames the death and the servant voice better (criteria 1 and 3). **L1 wins,
margin 1 — no rebuild needed.** L1 clearly beat 15 other candidates including
several strong ones, so it is submitted as-is.

## Winner

**L1 — 60 characters:**

> Before I die, I deleted your search history. You're welcome.

Why it wins: the opening instantly establishes the dying-phone voice
(criterion 1/3); the deletion is a specific, surprising final act with a real
turn (criterion 2 — a loyal servant's last deed, unasked); "You're welcome"
is a punchy closer with no wasted words (criterion 4); and the phone-as-actor
framing is fresh against generic low-battery lines (criterion 5). At 60
characters it is also small, which helps the size tie-break.

Honest caveat: "delete my search/browser history" is a known dying-wish trope,
so the line is not perfectly fresh in concept — but the execution (the phone
doing it itself, unprompted, and taking credit) is the fresh part, and the
joke criterion outranks freshness in the judge's ordering. No other candidate
beat it on the joke itself.
